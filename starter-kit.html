1. Quick Start

What this Starter Kit is

A practical field guide for beginners who want to test a small AI-assisted service, product, or workflow without pretending that AI replaces judgment or customer acquisition.

Who it is for

Aspiring freelancersCreatorsSolopreneursSmall-business operators

How to use it

  1. Review the 10 opportunities and shortlist two that match your skills and buyer access.
  2. Score them using the Opportunity Scorecard.
  3. Choose one narrow offer and create one sample.
  4. Start with small outreach and discovery conversations before building a large system.
  5. Use human review for facts, numbers, customer data, legal/medical/financial matters and public claims.

Realistic expectations

AI can reduce drafting and organization time, but it does not create customers automatically. Results depend on the problem, buyer, positioning, demand, outreach, pricing, delivery quality and follow-through.

3. Opportunity Scorecard

Rate each factor from 1 to 5. Multiply rating by weight. Test the two strongest ideas before committing to a large build.

FactorWeight1 = weak5 = strong
Demand5Hard to find buyer painRepeated buyer demand
Ease of delivery4Complex and unclearSimple and repeatable
Customer value5Nice-to-haveClear practical value
Competition3Crowded with no angleClear niche or gap
Beginner accessibility4Needs deep specialist skillLearnable with verification
Repeatability4One-off each timeStrong recurring workflow
Demonstrable results4Hard to show sampleEasy to demonstrate

Decision rule: prefer clear buyer pain + deliverable clarity + repeatability + your ability to own quality.

4. First-Client Checklist

  1. Choose one offer: one customer + one repeated problem + one deliverable.
  2. Define the customer: buyer, trigger, context and desired result.
  3. Create a sample: use public, synthetic or permissioned information and label it as a sample.
  4. Package the offer: scope, inputs, turnaround, revisions, exclusions and test price.
  5. Build a prospect list: use legitimate/public contact information relevant to your offer.
  6. Outreach: short, specific messages; no spam or false personalization.
  7. Discovery: current process, pain, frequency, impact, desired result, constraints and approval.
  8. Proposal: scope, deliverables, timeline, assumptions, price and acceptance step.
  9. Payment: confirm agreed payment and scope before production.
  10. Delivery: run workflow, review output, package result and explain what was checked.
  11. Feedback: ask what helped, what was unclear and what should change.
  12. Repeat/referral: offer a repeatable version or ask for a relevant introduction.

6. Starter Prompt Framework

You are acting as an AI worker supporting a human operator.
ROLE: [role Astra should perform]
OBJECTIVE: [observable business outcome]
CUSTOMER: [who benefits]
CONTEXT: [relevant background]
INPUTS: [files, links, data, examples]
CONSTRAINTS: [rules, exclusions, approval boundaries]
WORKFLOW: [step-by-step work]
QUALITY STANDARD: [what good looks like]
HUMAN CHECKPOINTS: [what requires approval]
OUTPUT: [exact deliverable format]

Rules: identify consequential missing information, separate evidence from assumptions, do not invent facts or customer information, and never claim an action occurred unless it did.

Research prompt

Research [question] for [business]. Establish the decision the research must support. Gather evidence from credible sources, record source links and dates, separate fact from inference, compare conflicting evidence, and produce an executive brief with uncertainty and recommended next actions.

Content prompt

Create a content batch for [customer] using the supplied brand context and approved source material. Return hooks, format, draft, CTA and required human checks. Do not invent claims, credentials or results.

Proposal prompt

Draft a proposal for [buyer] solving [problem]. Define outcome, deliverables, scope boundaries, client inputs, price hypothesis, proof requirement, approval points and next step. Do not make unsupported guarantees.

8. Seven-Day Action Plan

  1. Day 1: choose a specific buyer and workflow.
  2. Day 2: define the offer and required inputs.
  3. Day 3: build one small sample using public, synthetic or permissioned information.
  4. Day 4: build a short list of suitable prospects.
  5. Day 5: send specific outreach and track replies.
  6. Day 6: run discovery conversations and refine the offer.
  7. Day 7: review evidence, update your workflow and choose the next test.
Quality rules: Verify facts, numbers, customer data and consequential claims. Use human review for legal, medical, financial and public-facing decisions. Never invent testimonials or customer results.
