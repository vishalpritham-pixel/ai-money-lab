1. Quick Start

What this Starter Kit is

A practical field guide for beginners who want to test a small AI-assisted service, product, or workflow without pretending that AI replaces judgment or customer acquisition.

Who it is for

Aspiring freelancersCreatorsSolopreneursSmall-business operators

How to use it

  1. Review the 10 opportunities and shortlist two that match your skills and buyer access.
  2. Score them using the Opportunity Scorecard.
  3. Choose one narrow offer and create one sample.
  4. Start with small outreach and discovery conversations before building a large system.
  5. Use human review for facts, numbers, customer data, legal/medical/financial matters and public claims.

Realistic expectations

AI can reduce drafting and organization time, but it does not create customers automatically. Results depend on the problem, buyer, positioning, demand, outreach, pricing, delivery quality and follow-through.

3. Opportunity Scorecard

Rate each factor from 1 to 5. Multiply rating by weight. Test the two strongest ideas before committing to a large build.

FactorWeight1 = weak5 = strong
Demand5Hard to find buyer painRepeated buyer demand
Ease of delivery4Complex and unclearSimple and repeatable
Customer value5Nice-to-haveClear practical value
Competition3Crowded with no angleClear niche or gap
Beginner accessibility4Needs deep specialist skillLearnable with verification
Repeatability4One-off each timeStrong recurring workflow
Demonstrable results4Hard to show sampleEasy to demonstrate

Decision rule: prefer clear buyer pain + deliverable clarity + repeatability + your ability to own quality.

4. First-Client Checklist

  1. Choose one offer: one customer + one repeated problem + one deliverable.
  2. Define the customer: buyer, trigger, context and desired result.
  3. Create a sample: use public, synthetic or permissioned information and label it as a sample.
  4. Package the offer: scope, inputs, turnaround, revisions, exclusions and test price.
  5. Build a prospect list: use legitimate/public contact information relevant to your offer.
  6. Outreach: short, specific messages; no spam or false personalization.
  7. Discovery: current process, pain, frequency, impact, desired result, constraints and approval.
  8. Proposal: scope, deliverables, timeline, assumptions, price and acceptance step.
  9. Payment: confirm agreed payment and scope before production.
  10. Delivery: run workflow, review output, package result and explain what was checked.
  11. Feedback: ask what helped, what was unclear and what should change.
  12. Repeat/referral: offer a repeatable version or ask for a relevant introduction.

5. Five Starter Workflows

Research Brief

Problem: customer needs a concise answer before deciding.

Astra task: organize supplied sources and research notes.

Human review: verify facts, dates, sources and gaps.

Deliverable: research brief + source log.

Value: faster synthesis and clearer decision starting point.

Possible offer: Research Brief Sprint.

Lead Qualification

Problem: sales team has too many prospects and weak prioritization.

Astra task: structure fit criteria and extract public signals.

Human review: validate each record.

Deliverable: prioritized shortlist.

Value: cleaner sales queue.

Possible offer: Lead Research Pack.

Weekly KPI Report

Problem: owner has scattered numbers but no usable narrative.

Astra task: summarize supplied metrics.

Human review: reconcile totals and anomalies.

Deliverable: one-page report + actions/questions.

Value: faster review and communication.

Possible offer: Weekly Reporting Support.

Content Batch

Problem: business starts from scratch every week.

Astra task: draft hooks, captions and variants from approved inputs.

Human review: fact-check tone, claims and publishing choices.

Deliverable: 7-day batch or monthly queue.

Value: more consistent production.

Possible offer: Content Operations Pack.

Proposal Draft

Problem: seller needs a professional proposal quickly.

Astra task: turn discovery notes into a structured draft.

Human review: confirm scope, price, claims and terms.

Deliverable: client-ready proposal.

Value: less admin and more consistent sales documents.

Possible offer: Proposal System Setup.

6. Starter Prompt Framework

ROLE: Act as a [specific role].
OBJECTIVE: Produce [exact outcome].
CUSTOMER: The work is for [customer / decision].
CONTEXT: [verified facts only].
INPUTS: [files, notes, sources].
CONSTRAINTS: [rules, exclusions, format].
WORKFLOW: 1) inspect inputs 2) identify missing information 3) perform task 4) separate evidence from assumptions 5) prepare draft.
QUALITY STANDARD: [specific checks].
HUMAN CHECKPOINTS: [facts / numbers / claims / scope / approvals].
OUTPUT: [exact deliverable + structure].
DO NOT: invent facts, sources, customer details, results or evidence; do not claim an action happened unless it actually happened.

Prompt: Research Brief

Create a decision-ready research brief on [topic] using only supplied/verified sources. Extract key facts, implications, open questions and a source log. Label assumptions and end with a human verification checklist.

Prompt: Content Batch

Using the approved brand brief and source material, draft 7 posts for [audience]. Each needs a hook, body, CTA and visual angle. Do not add unsupported claims; flag items needing human fact-checking.

Prompt: Proposal Draft

Turn discovery notes into a proposal with problem, scope, deliverables, timeline, assumptions, price placeholders, revision policy and acceptance step. Flag missing commercial inputs.

Prompt: Data Report

Analyze the supplied metrics. Report changes, unusual movements, likely drivers as hypotheses, data-quality issues and management questions. Recalculate important totals where possible.

8. Seven-Day Action Plan

  1. Day 1: Choose one opportunity and one customer type.
  2. Day 2: Define deliverable, scope, turnaround, inputs, exclusions and test price.
  3. Day 3: Build one sample using public, synthetic or permissioned information.
  4. Day 4: Find 20 relevant prospects and record why each fits.
  5. Day 5: Send 5-10 specific outreach messages.
  6. Day 6: Run discovery conversations using a simple problem-to-outcome script.
  7. Day 7: Review responses, tighten the offer and decide whether to continue, change niche or replace the idea.

9. Quality & Safety Rules

  • Human review is mandatory for consequential work, including financial, legal, medical, HR, security and sensitive customer information.
  • Verify names, dates, numbers, claims, links and citations before external use.
  • Never use fake testimonials, fake results or invented customer stories.
  • Do not claim an AI tool completed an action when it only drafted or suggested something.
  • Collect only personal information necessary for the work.
  • Agree scope and payment terms before delivery.
